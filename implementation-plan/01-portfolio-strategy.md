# Portfolio strategy

[Index](README.md) · Design baseline, 18 September 2026 · Owner: Product Lead

## Customer and promise

Initial customer hypothesis: Portuguese SMEs with 20–200 employees, a small IT team or outsourced IT, and a named owner willing to run a measured pilot. This size range is a planning assumption to test, not a verified market conclusion. Prioritise professional services and technology-enabled SMEs for early discovery; avoid safety-critical production environments in the first releases.

Offer usable security workflows, understandable evidence, Portuguese support, and privacy-conscious operation. Do not advertise an autonomous SOC, guaranteed incident prevention, automatic compliance, or a proven training efficacy improvement before supporting evidence exists.

The initial paid product is Human Firewall. Optional secure connectivity supports deployment; it is no longer a mandatory bundled VPN offer. This supersedes the earlier combined access-and-training proposal.

## Portfolio outcomes

| Product | Buyer and user | Own-product differentiation hypothesis | Principal business measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human Firewall | SME owner/IT administrator; employees | Practical Portuguese scenarios, transparent adaptation, respectful feedback and simple administration | Paid renewal, usable learning outcomes, administration time |
| Security Operations | IT owner or service partner; investigator | One evidence chain from asset/software exposure to finding and reviewed remediation | Useful findings per investigation hour and renewal |
| Defense Box and Validation | IT provider or local infrastructure owner | Locally processed monitoring plus scoped, repeatable validation and remediation retests | Supportable deployments and verified remediation |
| Agent Security | Engineering/security lead; agent operators | Application-level tool authority and execution evidence connected to identity and model policy | Prevented policy violations with acceptable task success/cost |

Each product can be purchased, enabled, and removed independently. Shared identity, audit and deployment are platform capabilities, not compulsory cross-selling of other modules.

## Scope and operating principles

- One primary product stream; research prototypes cannot quietly become additional supported products.
- EU-managed isolated customer stacks first; edge processing for sensitive telemetry. On-premise commercial support follows repeatable packaging and demonstrated demand.
- Build product-specific intelligence, workflows, UX, integrations and evidence. Reuse cryptography, identity standards, scanners, sensors and execution runtimes where appropriate.
- Use validated rules as initial baselines. Promote ML or agentic behaviour only when it improves a measured outcome at acceptable cost and risk.
- Learning profiles are not employee risk rankings. No biometric emotion inference or personality-based access decisions in client products.
- Guided response precedes approved automation. Models propose; deterministic policy and authorised people control consequential actions.
- Open-source deliverables must be useful and reproducible. Paid offerings fund operation, enterprise administration, integrations and support.

## Wider vision preserved

The longer-term portfolio retains UEBA/graph research, local AI, explainability, controlled adversarial testing, federation, industrial/IoT monitoring, recovery exercises, insurer evidence export, and national-framework mappings. Industrial pilots and international expansion require partner readiness and capacity after the core releases. They are not commitments to launch additional products inside the same lean-team budget.

## Capacity and authority

Roles: Product Lead (PL), Platform/Security Lead (PS), Application Engineer (AE), Research Lead (RL). People may hold multiple roles; production-impacting changes still require a second reviewer. Independent assessment is contracted for each materially new product surface before general availability.

Planning baseline: three full-time equivalents, approximately 60% primary product, 20% research/discovery, 20% maintenance/support. This is 1.8 product FTE, not three full parallel teams. At two contributors, reduce scope or extend dependent milestones; at four, add reliability/customer integration capacity before parallel product launches. If support exceeds 20% for two consecutive sprints, pause feature expansion and remediate the operating burden.

The Product Lead owns scope and pricing; PS owns technical release approval; RL owns research claims. Legal applicability and upstream commercial terms require qualified external confirmation before the relevant commitment. These checks do not block unrelated engineering.

## Decision criteria

At every release gate assess customer use, repeatable delivery, security evidence, operating cost and research honesty. Recruitment, conversion, and effectiveness targets are hypotheses. Failed commercial validation triggers packaging/scope revision; failed safety or isolation tests block release. [Roadmap](08-roadmap-backlog.md) defines sequencing and [production handbook](12-production-handbook.md) defines evidence.
